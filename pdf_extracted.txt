MINI PROJECT REPORT
Agentic AI Based Smart Pharmacy Management System
ABSTRACT
The healthcare sector requires accurate and efficient management of medicines to ensure patient
safety and proper treatment. Pharmacies play a crucial role in dispensing medicines prescribed by
doctors and maintaining records of medicine inventory and patient transactions. However, many
small and medium-scale pharmacies still rely on manual processes for managing inventory,
verifying prescriptions, generating bills, and maintaining patient records. These manual processes
increase the workload of pharmacists and can lead to errors such as incorrect medicine dispensing,
inventory mismanagement, and delays in service.
With advancements in Artificial Intelligence and web technologies, intelligent systems can now
assist pharmacists in performing these tasks more efficiently. This project proposes an Agentic AI
Based Smart Pharmacy Management System  that integrates multiple AI agents to automate
pharmacy operations. The system uses a chat-based interface through which pharmacists can enter
medicine orders in natural language. AI agents analyze the request, validate prescription
requirements, check inventory availability, and process the order automatically.
The system also generates bills, updates medicine stock levels, and sends alerts when inventory is
running low. The architecture includes several specialized agents such as the Ordering Agent, Safety
Agent, Inventory Refill Agent, Notification Agent, Observability Agent, and Recommendation
Agent. Each agent performs a specific task and collaborates with other agents to complete the
workflow.
The application is built using modern technologies including React for the user interface, Node.js 
and Express.js for backend services, MongoDB Atlas for data storage, and OpenAI’s SDK model 
for intelligent processing. The system aims to reduce manual workload, improve accuracy, and 
enhance efficiency in pharmacy management while maintaining transparency and safety in 
medicine dispensing.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
The healthcare sector plays a crucial role in ensuring the well-being of society by providing medical
services, diagnosis, and treatment to patients. Pharmacies are an essential component of the 
healthcare system as they act as the final point where prescribed medicines are dispensed to 
patients. Pharmacists are responsible for providing correct medicines, ensuring proper dosage, 
maintaining medicine inventory, and keeping accurate records of transactions. In addition to 
dispensing medicines, pharmacists must also verify prescriptions, manage stock levels, monitor 
medicine expiry dates, and generate bills for each purchase.
In many pharmacies, particularly small and medium-scale establishments, these processes are still 
performed manually or with limited digital tools. Manual systems require pharmacists to check 
inventory records physically, interpret handwritten prescriptions, and update stock records after 
each transaction. This manual workflow often leads to inefficiencies, delays, and potential errors. 
For example, pharmacists may accidentally dispense incorrect medicines, miscalculate bills, or fail 
to update inventory accurately. Such issues not only affect pharmacy operations but may also have 
serious consequences for patient safety.
With the rapid advancement of digital technologies, especially in the fields of artificial intelligence 
and cloud computing, it has become possible to automate many of these routine tasks. Artificial 
Intelligence (AI) has the ability to analyze data, understand natural language, and assist in decision-
making processes. AI technologies are increasingly being adopted in healthcare applications such as
disease diagnosis, medical imaging analysis, patient monitoring, and healthcare management 
systems.
One of the emerging paradigms in artificial intelligence is Agentic AI , which involves the use of 
multiple intelligent agents working collaboratively to perform complex tasks. Each agent is 
designed to perform a specific function and interact with other agents in order to achieve a common
objective. Agent-based systems are particularly useful in scenarios where multiple tasks must be 
handled simultaneously with coordination and automation.
In the context of pharmacy management, Agentic AI can significantly enhance operational 
efficiency by automating tasks such as order processing, prescription validation, inventory 
monitoring, and alert generation. By implementing multiple AI agents, the system can analyze

1.2 Problem Statement
Pharmacy management is a critical process in healthcare operations, as it directly impacts patient 
treatment and medication safety. Traditional pharmacy systems often rely on manual methods or 
basic software tools that focus only on billing and record storage. These systems lack intelligent 
automation capabilities and require pharmacists to perform multiple tasks manually.
One of the major problems in traditional pharmacy operations is the manual processing of medicine 
orders. Pharmacists must interpret prescriptions, check medicine availability, calculate prices, and 
update inventory records after each transaction. This process can become complex when handling a 
large number of patients or when dealing with extensive medicine inventories.
Another challenge is the verification of prescription requirements. Certain medicines can only be 
dispensed when a valid prescription is provided by a licensed medical practitioner. In manual 
systems, pharmacists must carefully verify these requirements to avoid dispensing restricted 
medicines without authorization. This process increases workload and may lead to mistakes.
Inventory management is another major issue in pharmacy operations. Without proper monitoring 
systems, pharmacies may experience stock shortages or maintain excess inventory that eventually 
expires. Stock shortages can delay patient treatment, while overstocking can result in financial 
losses due to expired medicines.
Additionally, traditional pharmacy systems do not provide transparency in decision-making 
processes. When errors occur, it becomes difficult to determine what caused the issue or how it 
could be prevented in the future. The lack of intelligent monitoring and logging mechanisms limits 
the ability to analyze system behavior.
Therefore, there is a need for an intelligent pharmacy management system that can assist 
pharmacists in performing routine tasks, reduce manual workload, and improve decision accuracy.

2.2 Review of Existing Systems
2.2.1 Traditional Pharmacy Management Systems
Traditional pharmacy management systems are commonly used in retail pharmacies to maintain 
records of medicines, sales transactions, and inventory levels. These systems typically include 
modules for billing, stock management, and customer record maintenance. Most of these 
applications rely on basic database operations where pharmacists manually enter medicine details 
and update inventory after every transaction.
Although these systems improve record keeping compared to manual paper-based methods, they 
still require significant human intervention. Pharmacists must manually verify prescriptions, search 
for medicines in the database, and update stock levels. In many cases, these systems do not provide 
automatic alerts for low stock levels or expired medicines. Additionally, they do not support natural 
language interaction, which means pharmacists must navigate through multiple menus and forms to 
process orders.
Another limitation of traditional systems is the lack of intelligent decision support. These systems 
cannot analyze prescription data, recommend alternative medicines, or detect potential safety issues.
As a result, pharmacists still need to rely on their own judgment and manual verification for critical 
tasks.
2.2.2 AI-Based Inventory Management Systems
Recent studies have explored the use of artificial intelligence techniques for improving inventory 
management in pharmaceutical supply chains. Machine learning algorithms can analyze historical 
sales data and predict future medicine demand. These predictive models help pharmacies maintain 
optimal inventory levels and reduce the risk of stock shortages.
AI-based inventory management systems use techniques such as demand forecasting, pattern 
recognition, and predictive analytics to determine reorder points for medicines. These systems can 
automatically generate restocking alerts when inventory falls below a predefined threshold. This 
approach improves supply chain efficiency and ensures the availability of essential medicines.
However, many of these systems focus only on inventory optimization and do not address other 
pharmacy management challenges such as prescription validation, order processing, and patient

training processes, which may not be feasible for small pharmacies.

Artificial intelligence has been widely applied in healthcare systems to assist medical professionals 
in diagnosis, treatment planning, and patient monitoring. AI-powered systems can analyze large 
volumes of medical data and provide insights that help healthcare providers make better decisions.
In pharmacy-related applications, AI has been used to detect medication errors, analyze drug 
interactions, and recommend treatment options based on patient history. Natural language 
processing technologies allow systems to interpret textual information such as medical prescriptions

Despite these advancements, many healthcare AI systems focus primarily on clinical decision 
support rather than operational management of pharmacy services. They often require specialized 
medical datasets and complex infrastructure. Furthermore, these systems may not provide simple 
user interfaces that pharmacists can easily use in their daily operations

The system should allow pharmacists to register new patients by entering their personal details such
